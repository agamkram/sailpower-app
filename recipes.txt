SailPower model notes
Wind 10 m/s, sail 2×5 m, 11.6 kg chassis plus a 16 kg sail, rail 749 N,
track 10 m. Figures are three steady cycles after the first, averaged over the
whole cycle including time at rest.

The rotation plan
-----------------
The six named recipes are gone. They were never six rotation rules: they mixed
three knobs at once, and the one that won on paper was another recipe at a
different cruise speed with identical turn timing. Three of them were also
handed a widened braking zone so they had room to turn in, so "turn two
seconds early" never meant that.

A plan is now two numbers, and cruise speed is a spec, not part of the plan.

  turn      how long the 90 degree slew takes
  turnLead  where that slew sits relative to arrival, as a fraction of its own
            length
            0   the slew begins once the cart is stopped
            1   the slew finishes exactly as the cart stops

turnLead was seconds at first, and that was wrong. The cart cruises out at
2.4 m/s and comes home at 8, so the same number of seconds is a short distance
one way and a long one the other. A one second lead left the sail wide open
four metres from home with the motor still hauling against it, and the setting
that was meant to read "slightly early" collapsed to five watts. Tied to the
turn instead, it means the same thing in both directions and cannot ask for the
sail to be open before the cart has arrived.

Braking starts where the stopping distance says it must. The lead reaches back
into the cruise, so a long lead really does turn the sail while it is still
making power, and the numbers show what that costs.

The sail angle is a command, not a commitment. Inside the lead it drives toward
the angle the stroke ends on, outside it drives back to the angle the stroke
runs at. A one-way turn meant a sail that opened too early kept opening while
the wind drove the cart backwards, until it sat pinned against the far cap half
open with nothing left to try.

Braking only promises to stop the cart, not to stop it at the cap, and in a
strong wind it stops short. The last stretch into each cap is closed at a creep
speed instead. The stop-at-the-cap curve stays in charge until the cart is in
that stretch or already slower than the creep. Switching at 0.5 m/s a metre out
left it cruising into the bumper; flooring the remaining gap at 0.12 m then
asked for a gentle last decimetre and charged the hit.

turnLead lives inside Best. It is not a spec slider.

Sliders that fit each other
---------------------------
The sliders were ranged one at a time, so they did not describe one machine.
Rail force stopped at 3000 N while the biggest sail in the strongest wind
pushes about 17600, which is how a wide sail came to be braked by the end stop
instead of the coils. Turn went down to 0.2 s for every sail, and an 8 m plate
needs 53 kN m to move that fast. Return speed went to 16 m/s with no regard
for motoring force, which droops with back-EMF and is gone by 22 m/s.

The sail and the wind are free. The cart under the sail and the rail that
holds it are sized to them, so neither is a slider.

  chassis    the lightest carbon bogie that can carry this sail in this wind.
             The plate's own mass is separate. The bogie is a 60 mm carbon
             tube worked at 200 MPa, a light slewing ring, the guide wheels,
             and a short magnet mover at 4 kg per kN of rail force. At the
             default that is 11.6 kg under a 16 kg sail.
  rail force the push that holds the sail standing still, and never below the
             push that shifts the whole cart at 1 m/s². At the default, 749 N.
  turn       no quicker than the slew drive can manage at 150 N m per m2 of
             sail. That works out at 0.075 s per metre of width whatever the
             drive is sized to, so 0.15 s for a 2 m sail and 0.6 s for an 8 m
  return     no faster than the coils can still hold against the edge-on drag
             with most of the force left over as margin

Best only offers plans inside those ends, and it has to beat the plan it
started from on the same ledger. scripts/range-check.mjs replays what a range
input does to its own value when the ends move, checks that what is left is
still a machine that completes a cycle, and refuses a Best that loses watts.

The cycle budget is what the sim will wait for before calling a plan no plan.
It was a flat forty seconds, which reported a 40 m track crossed at a crawl as
impossible rather than as bad. It now follows the track and the speeds, capped
at 400 s. Searching uses one cycle at a 0.05 s step, which ranks candidates to
within a watt of the full run and keeps Best to a few seconds on the slowest
machine in the space.

The cart is captive: the bogie grips the rail from above, below and both
sides, so the overturning moment from the sail is a guide-wheel load and not a
tipping failure. At the default it is about sixteen times what the weight alone
would resist. That load is still not charged for, apart from the rolling it
adds.

Forces and losses
-----------------
Carried:

  aero        flat plate, cos^2 law on yaw, plus edge and skin terms
  Cd          from harvest. At one-third of wind speed the share kept is
              Cd×4/27, so Cd = harvest × 27/4. 18% is Cd 1.22. No slider.
  yaw inertia m*w^2/12 about the vertical pivot, width squared
  added air   rho*(pi/4)*c^2*b with a slenderness factor, acting as cos^2 of
              yaw. Conservative, so it moves timing and peak force, not the watt-seconds
  slew work   spin-up through the motor, aero torque against the loaded plate,
              and no recovery on spin-down because a geared slew drive is not
              back-drivable. slewRegen buys that back if the drive is direct
  droop       motoring force falls as back-EMF rises and reaches zero at
              vNoLoad. Braking is thermal only, so it keeps the full rated force
  copper      cuMax*(F/fMax)^2 at every speed, not only while parked
  iron        ironRef*(v/10)^2
  inverter    98% each way. Copper and iron are already counted, so this is
              only the power electronics. Not a slider.
  harvest     sets the face Cd. The books credit the mechanical power through
              the coils, after the inverter, only while the coils are
              generating. A flat 18% of the wind paid that rate while the cart
              sat still and while the sail was edge-on, so a slower return
              looked like more watts and Best stretched the cycle on purpose.
  rolling     steel rollers on the rail, 0.002 of the load. The load is the
              cart's weight plus the sail's push through the guide rollers.
              Not a slider.
  end stops   watt-seconds absorbed by a stop are charged, not deleted

Budget at the default, in watts. Trip, Last, Save, and Best all read this
ledger. The opening plan is already Best for this machine.

  made          861
  motor home   -263
  slew drive    -54
  copper+iron   -51
  end stops      -1
  net           492     cycle 5.6 s

Not carried: wind shear and gusts, unsteady aerodynamics beyond added mass,
side force and the overturning moment on the guide wheels, sail flex, thermal
derating, and any money cost for the structure. Its mass is in the cart.

Sail shape
----------
The model used to see area and nothing else, so it could not tell a 5x2 sail
from a 2x5. Harvest now sets the same Cd on every shape, so the gap is yaw
inertia alone. At a constant 10 m2, with the plan solved for each shape:

  sail        AR     Cd   I kg m2   turn s   net W
  1.00x10.00 10.0  1.215      1.3     0.20     517
  2.00x5.00   2.5  1.215      5.3     0.30     492
  3.16x3.16   1.0  1.215     13.3     0.50     454
  5.00x2.00   2.5  1.215     33.3     0.70     402
  10.00x1.00 10.0  1.215    133.3     1.00     299

1x10 and 10x1 have identical area and identical Cd and differ by 218 W. All of
it is yaw inertia. Tall and narrow is cheap to turn. The best slew time tracks
inertia, from 0.20 s for the slender sail to 1.00 s for the wide one.

Cruise speed
------------
The optimum outbound is about 0.24 of wind speed, not the one third the old
defaults used. Drag-device theory puts the peak at U/3 for the power stroke
alone, but the return trip and the time at rest are fixed overhead, so a slower
outbound spreads that overhead across more generating time. On the default
machine 0.24 nets 492 W; one third nets 456 W. It moves with the other specs,
which is why Best exists rather than a hardcoded number.

Above rated wind
----------------
The rail grows with the hold force, so a 10 m2 sail does not out-push a fixed
1000 N rail. At 30 m/s the default shape sits on a 6742 N rail and nets about
9690 W. Face-on spare brake still vanishes as the cart slows, because the rail
is sized to the rest load and no higher, so the far cap is a harder stop and
those watt-seconds are charged. The sail is not turned to shed it. Turning it
early made sail width start a second rotation.

On the way home the arrival slew stops at the angle the motor can still creep
against. Opening past that used to blow the cart back down the track, and the
turn was then commanded shut, so the last stretch home was taken edge-on at
about 0.05 m/s. The rest of the turn happens at the cap. Opening earlier still
costs motor work. It no longer stalls the cycle.

Sanity
------
Made on the default machine is 12% of the wind, which is 67% of the 18%
drag-device peak the harvest slider names. A long track helps: 8 m/s on 40 m
reaches 72% of that peak. Light carts can poke a few percent above the face
ceiling because braking returns stored kinetic energy and the skin and edge
terms sit outside that Cd.

Across 560 combinations of wind 4-30 m/s, five sail shapes from 2 to 16 m2,
track 8-40 m and the four turnLead values, every one finishes a cycle. The
rail is always at least the hold force. 99 of them still charge the end stop
above 5 W, almost all of that on the way out in strong wind, where face-on
spare brake runs out as the cart slows.

The four corners that used to time out were one machine at each rotation
preset. A 2 m2 sail in a 14 m/s wind on a 40 m track with a 300 N rail pushes
280 N face-on, so the rail can hold it. A floor at 40 N had called a 30 N
spare no stop at all, the cart crept the whole track, and the sampler gave up
at 160 s. Thirty newtons stops that cart in a few metres. The rail is now
sized to the hold, so that machine is not offered a 300 N rail.

Every commanded rotation takes exactly the turn time.
