WindCart rotation plan
Wind 10 m/s, sail 5x2 m, 40 kg, rail 1000 N, track 20 m. Three steady cycles
after the first. Average is net energy over cycle time, including time at rest.

The six named recipes are gone. They were not six rotation rules: they mixed
three knobs at once, and the one that won on paper, "slower outbound" at 497 W,
was another recipe at a different cruise speed with identical turn timing.

A rotation plan is now two numbers, and cruise speed is a spec slider, not part
of the plan.

  turn      how long the 90 degree slew takes, seconds
  turnLead  how many seconds before the cart reaches rest the slew begins
            0            turn only after the cart has stopped
            = turn       the slew finishes exactly as the cart stops
            > turn       the slew finishes while the cart is still moving

Braking now starts where the stopping distance says it must. No plan is handed
extra room to turn in, which is what three of the old recipes were quietly given.

Presets vary turnLead alone, so the selector compares turn timing and nothing
else. At turn 1 s, outFrac 1/3:

  Turn once stopped       lead 0.00    443 W
  Finish as it stops      lead 0.25    460 W   <- default
  Turn while slowing      lead 0.50    453 W
  Turn before the brake   lead 1.00   -120 W

Going edge-on before the brake throws away the end of the power stroke, and
coming face-on early means motoring home into a loaded sail. The cliff is real.

Sail shape is now part of the physics
-------------------------------------
The model used to see area and nothing else, so it could not tell a 5x2 sail
from a 2x5 one. Three shape effects are now carried:

  Cd        Hoerner's fit for a rectangular plate, 1.1 + 0.02(AR + 1/AR).
            A square plate is the bluffest shape at Cd 1.14; slenderness
            raises it to about 1.30 by AR 10. The Cd spec is now a trim
            around that, not the value itself.
  inertia   yaw inertia m*w^2/12 about the vertical pivot. Width squared, so
            this is the term that separates wide sails from tall ones.
  added air rho*(pi/4)*c^2*b with a slenderness factor. Conservative, so it
            moves timing and peak force rather than net energy.

Slew work is charged: spin the sail up to rate through the motor, recover on
the way down through the generator, plus the aero torque turned against while
the plate is still loaded. Mass is now chassis plus sail, where the sail is
1.6 kg/m^2, so growing the sail adds weight as well as area.

At a constant 10 m2 and a 1 s slew:

  sail        AR     Cd   I kg m2   peak Nm   slew W   net W
  1.00x10.00 10.0  1.302      1.3         8       10     438
  2.00x5.00   2.5  1.158      5.3        34       17     383
  3.16x3.16   1.0  1.140     13.3        84       27     370
  5.00x2.00   2.5  1.158     33.3       209       45     364
  10.00x1.00 10.0  1.302    133.3       838      105     368

1x10 and 10x1 have identical area and identical Cd and differ by 70 W. All of
it is yaw inertia. Tall and narrow wins: it is the bluffer shape and it is
cheap to turn.

The slew optimum is now interior and depends on shape, which is the point:
5x2 peaks near 0.5 s, 2x5 near 0.3 s, 10x1 near 0.75 s. Asking a 10 m wide
sail for a 0.2 s turn costs more than the machine makes.

Still open
----------
1. Cruise speed is worth more than rotation. Holding the plan fixed and
   sweeping outbound speed spans 378 to 511 W, and the best is about 2.2 m/s,
   not the one third of wind speed the defaults use. Classic drag-device theory
   puts the peak at U/3 for the power stroke alone, but the return trip and the
   time at rest are fixed overhead, so a slower outbound spreads that overhead
   across more generating time. The optimum moves with the other specs, so it
   wants solving, not hardcoding.

2. Control authority. The coils saturate near 1000 N, which the sail passes at
   about 12 m/s of wind. Past that the cycle is 75% force-limited and the
   numbers describe the clip, not the wind. Above about 16 m/s it deadlocks:
   a real machine would feather to shed load and this controller only turns at
   the ends.

3. The machine model is still idealised. fMax is flat against speed where a
   real linear generator droops, copper loss is charged only below 0.08 m/s
   with a flat 85% everywhere else, and rolling resistance follows weight
   while ignoring the aero reaction through the guide wheels, which runs about
   twice the weight. End stops also delete 1 to 3 percent of net energy by
   clamping velocity to zero, which quietly rewards configs that cannot brake.
